# P3.2 - Pause taxonomy and per-pause schedules

P3.2 adds the four live LXR pause kinds, their exact enabled work-bucket sets, and the coordinator
that interrupts concurrent packets, suspends the runtime, executes one ordered P3.1 cycle, resets
the buckets, and resumes preserved concurrent work. It does not implement a collector, trigger
policy, reference-count mutation, SATB tracing, sweeping, evacuation, root scanning, or pinning.

## Paper requirements

The paper is Zhao, Blackburn, and McKinley, *Low-Latency, High-Throughput Garbage Collection
(Extended Version)*, arXiv:2210.17175, 1 Nov 2022.

- Section 3.2.1, PDF pp.5-6 requires every RC epoch to apply all increments before decrements and
  moves lazy decrements after the brief STW pause.
- Section 3.2.2, PDF pp.6-7 permits a SATB trace to span multiple interrupting RC epochs.
- Section 3.3.1, PDF p.7 places young sweeping in the STW work and mature decrement/sweeping work
  concurrently after it.
- Section 3.5, PDF p.9 requires parallel increment/decrement work and work stealing.
- Table 1, PDF p.2 shows LXR GC pauses longer than G1 at every reported percentile while query
  latency is better, under the caption "Short GC pauses do not assure low latency".
- Table 7, PDF p.13 reports LXR-only pause rates and p50/p95 values at a 2x heap. It is
  characterization, not a comparative pause target.

## Pause taxonomy

`LxrPauseKind` contains exactly the four live LXR pause kinds in their relative oracle order:

```text
Full
RefCount
InitialMark
FinalMark
```

The source enum is `304ce69d:src/plan/immix/mod.rs:12-17`. Its `FullDefrag` case is not included:
LXR's dispatch at `304ce69d:src/plan/lxr/global.rs:225-231` marks that case unreachable. Names,
ordinals, and cardinality have compile-time and executable checks.

## Exact schedule matrix

The structured authority is `P3.2/mechanism-spec.json`. The active HEAD configuration has lazy
decrements, so `STWRCDecsAndSweep` is disabled for every kind except `Full`.

| stage | `Full` | `RefCount` | `InitialMark` | `FinalMark` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `FinishConcurrentWork` | enabled | enabled | enabled | enabled |
| `Initial` / `LxrRCProcessIncs` | enabled | enabled | enabled | enabled |
| `Prepare` | enabled | **disabled** | enabled | enabled |
| `Closure` | enabled | **disabled** | **disabled** | enabled |
| `Release` | enabled | enabled | enabled | enabled |
| `STWRCDecsAndSweep` / `LxrRCProcessDecs` | enabled | **disabled** | **disabled** | **disabled** |
| `Final` | enabled | enabled | enabled | enabled |

The disable policy is `304ce69d:src/plan/lxr/global.rs:696-719`; the per-kind packet placement is
`304ce69d:src/plan/lxr/global.rs:722-787`; the bucket order and RC aliases are
`304ce69d:src/scheduler/work_bucket.rs:355-469`.

`Unconstrained` remains always open. `Concurrent` and `ConcurrentResumable` do not execute while the
pause is active. Disabled STW stages contain no packets and advance as absent stages in the ordered
chain.

## Coordinator protocol

### Prepare

`PreparePause` is legal only from idle. It validates the kind, installs the exact stage mask while
all STW buckets are closed and empty, and moves packets submitted to enabled stages into P3.1's next
generation. Submission to a disabled STW stage fails explicitly with `StageDisabled`.

### Interrupt

`ConcurrentResumable` closes admission and stops taking new packets first. A packet already
executing reaches its packet boundary; queued packets retain scheduler ownership. Ordinary
`Concurrent` work remains runnable until it drains, then closes atomically under the scheduler
lock. The transition also preserves P3.1's `Unconstrained` drain and executing-count preconditions.

The secondary reference re-enables a concurrent bucket only when it has queued work. P3.2 follows
that rule: after reset, preserved resumable packets reopen their bucket; empty concurrent buckets
remain disabled.

### Suspend and execute

The coordinator stores one runtime adapter at initialization. The real adapter delegates to
CoreCLR's existing `GCToEEInterface::SuspendEE` and `RestartEE` contract; the synthetic adapter is
used by native validation and the scenario benchmark. There is no per-pause capability branch and no
new GC, EE, JIT-EE, managed, or NativeAOT interface.

After interruption, the adapter suspends the runtime and P3.1 opens `FinishConcurrentWork`. Each
stage opens and completes once in ordinal order. A race that makes the final drain recheck fail
restarts the runtime and restores the pre-pause concurrent state rather than leaving the EE
suspended.

### Complete and reset

Completion waits for `Final`, requires zero outstanding/executing/queued STW work, restores every STW
bucket to closed and enabled, clears the per-cycle traces, and reopens only concurrent buckets that
hold preserved work. Concurrent packets become schedulable before the real adapter resumes mutators,
matching `9625c174:src/scheduler/scheduler.rs:797-809`.

Illegal duplicate prepare/start/complete/reset transitions fail explicitly. Packet ownership,
generation, stealing, and drain accounting remain P3.1 responsibilities.

## Integration boundary

### Integrated deferred-generation lifetime

When the P3.3 deferred queues are enabled, STW completion no longer resets the
cycle before resume. The coordinator reopens preserved concurrent work, restarts
the EE, records the actual mutator resume, and publishes the sealed generation.
The next `PreparePause` retires the previous cycle only after its active deferred
work and P3.4 completion ownership have drained. A busy retirement rejects the
new pause without suspending the EE or changing the next-generation producer.

The non-deferred coordinator retains its reset-before-resume protocol. The
combined direct validator exercises three alternating queue generations,
publication rejection inside the resume callback, lazy work that remains
executing after `CompletePause`, and completion-zero ordering. Historical
P3.2 source/evidence records below describe the original non-deferred slice.

Product sources are compiled only with
`CLR_CMAKE_ENABLE_LXR_PAUSE_COORDINATOR_TEST=1`. That option also includes the P3.1 scheduler
objects, then adds the P3.2 schedule, coordinator, and one validation export. It defaults off.

Normal Workstation and Server builds contain no P3.2 source object, call, branch, or export.
The enabled test build exposes `GC_LxrPauseCoordinatorTest_Run` in linked and standalone GCs.

No `gcinterface.h`, `gcinterface.ee.h`, JIT/JIT-EE, public managed API, or NativeAOT contract is
changed.

## Responsibility boundary

P3.2 owns:

- pause names and exact bucket masks;
- legal pause transitions;
- concurrent packet interruption/resume;
- initialization-selected runtime suspension;
- per-kind count and duration instrumentation.

P3.3 owns production trigger and selection policy, including the eventual mapping from allocation
pressure and induced collections to these pause kinds.

Later RC/SATB steps own packet bodies, recursive decrements, 2/4/8-bit RC semantics, side metadata,
sweeping, tracing, object/root scanning, forwarding, evacuation, weak processing, and pinning. P3.2
does not assume the default two-bit `MAX_REF_COUNT == 3`.

## Validation and benchmark boundary

The Windows and Linux native validator checks exact names, ordinals, masks, enabled/forbidden
submission, legal and illegal transitions, one-open/one-complete traces, reset defaults, drain
accounting, packet-boundary interruption, queued resume, and post-suspension rollback.

The runtime smoke invokes all four schedules through the real adapter in linked and standalone
Workstation and Server configurations.

The benchmark bridge uses the synthetic adapter. It drives all ten P0.4 scenarios and records a
separate `pauseSchedule` object containing per-kind samples. `scheduled` and paired `same-code`
policies execute identical work; `null` crosses the same managed/native boundary without a schedule;
`injected` pairs a normal schedule with one carrying a fixed five-millisecond `Initial` packet and
reports their in-process delta.

These are scheduler/synthetic pause measurements. Existing harness throughput and
application-observed latency remain separately labelled context. No result is a complete LXR
collector measurement or paper-parity claim.

## Candidate validation and measurement

The reviewed candidate passes:

- 355/355 direct pause checks on Windows x64;
- 355/355 direct pause checks on Linux x64;
- the inherited P3.1 validator, 642/642 checks;
- 59/59 dependency-free harness tests;
- enabled Windows x64 Debug and Linux x64 Debug `clr.runtime` builds;
- linked and standalone Workstation and Server runtime smoke on Windows and Linux, eight rows;
- a default-off Windows build with no `GC_LxrPauseCoordinatorTest_Run` export;
- one Windows combined-feature build with the P1.1, P1.4, P1.5, P1.6, P2.2, P3.1, and P3.2
  validation options enabled together.

The x86 and ARM64 portable translation units compile with their target MSVC toolchains. Full target
builds compile every P3.2 linked and standalone object, including `lxrpausecoordinatortest.cpp`,
before failing in pre-existing cross-component work:

- x86 reaches the known Windows SDK `_CONTEXT` mismatch, whose selected definition has no
  `Eax`/`Ecx`/`Eip` members;
- ARM64 reaches the x64-host debug cross-component assembler and rejects ARMASM `GBLS`/`GBLA`
  directives in generated `floatconversion.asm`.

Neither failure is in a P3.2 translation unit. ARM64 execution hardware is unavailable.

The candidate scenario matrix has 960/960 passing per-kind rows:

Two smoke matrices are retained as superseded evidence rather than rewritten. The first used
sub-tick `sleep_for` work and compared injected and normal durations across processes; Windows timer
rounding and process drift made that control insensitive. The second paired injection in-process
and used high-resolution busy work, but its gate incorrectly assigned rows to PowerShell's reserved
`$null` variable. The final policy fixes the measurement and the gate, not the observed rows.

The first immutable-source matrix at `12e4f7251f18e267dbd3d90088fb823ebd57fb96` retained a third
negative result: 79/80 sensitivity rows passed, while one `cyclic-garbage` Workstation
`InitialMark` row reported a 3.826 ms injected delta against the 4 ms floor. The superseding policy
does not rerun the same sparse sample until it passes. It raises the fixed per-kind sample count from
2 to 8 and alternates injected/base order inside the same native call, preserving the five
millisecond injection and all predeclared bounds.

The first matrix from source `e8b9f3fdd870c7c838f5d6f067d6d363fd07ad9c` then retained another
negative result: all three `lifecycle-semantics` Server/null launches exhausted the 2x calibrated
heap while the slower scheduled policies survived. That is a policy-dependent allocation-volume
confound, not a pause-schedule result. The superseding fixed matrix moves every policy and scenario
uniformly to the existing calibrated 6x heap axis. It does not special-case the failed scenario or
discard the failed rows.
