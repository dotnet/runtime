# P3.3 - Deferred concurrent bucket

P3.3 extends the P3.1 scheduler with the double-buffered publication boundary used for work that is
created before or during a stop-the-world collection but must execute only after mutators resume.
The implementation remains collector-internal, is built only by an opt-in validation switch, and
does not add a runtime, EE, JIT, or managed contract.

## Paper requirement

The paper is Zhao, Blackburn, and McKinley, *Low-Latency, High-Throughput Garbage Collection
(Extended Version)*, arXiv:2210.17175, 1 Nov 2022.

- Section 3.2.1, PDF pp.5-6 requires every RC epoch to apply all increments before decrements.
- Figure 2 and “Optimization: Lazy Decrements,” PDF p.6 place lazy decrements immediately after a
  brief STW pause, concurrent with mutators, and ahead of SATB work.
- “Optimization: Lazy Reclamation,” PDF p.7 resumes mutators after young sweeping, then performs
  decrements and mature sweeping concurrently.
- Table 7 and its discussion, PDF p.13 report unfinished lazy decrements entering a later pause only
  for xalan (22%), lusearch (5%), and avrora (1%). These are characterization data, not a threshold
  for this synthetic scheduler substrate.

Table 1, PDF p.2 remains the only comparative pause table. LXR pauses are longer than G1 at every
listed percentile while application query latency is better. P3.3 therefore characterizes moved
pause work; it does not claim a collector pause win.

## Queue representation and ownership

`LxrWorkScheduler` owns exactly two deferred queue sets. Each set has a prioritized FIFO and a
normal FIFO. Three indices describe their role:

- `producerDeferredIndex` is the only side that accepts deferred submissions;
- `sealedDeferredIndex` is the completed generation waiting for publication;
- `activeDeferredIndex` is the only side workers may poll.

Before the first cycle, side 0 is the empty active side and side 1 is the generation-1 producer
side. A deferred submission transfers packet ownership to the scheduler, records the producer
generation and preferred worker, but does not increment `Unconstrained` outstanding work or wake a
worker.

P3.1's scheduler mutex is the synchronization boundary. Packet payloads are immutable. The mutex
release after submission/sealing/publication happens-before a worker's locked poll, and condition
notification follows publication. No separate relaxed-atomic queue protocol is used.

## Seal, resume, and publication

When `Final` drains, all workers are parked and P3.1 has already proved current `Unconstrained` work
drained. Under the scheduler mutex P3.3:

1. seals the current producer side for the completed generation;
2. converts the old, empty active side into the next-generation producer side;
3. records no active deferred side.

This is the local equivalent of the oracle's pre-resume queue detachment. Producers that linearize
before sealing belong to the completed generation. Producers that linearize after sealing belong to
the next generation and cannot leak into the queue about to be published.

The App-approved integration uses an explicit two-step boundary:

1. `ResumeConcurrentWork` records that mutators have resumed;
2. `PublishDeferredConcurrentWork(generation)` performs the flip.

Publication fails closed unless the generation is complete, sealed, resumed, and not previously
published. The flip installs the sealed side as active, transfers its cardinality into P3.1's
`Unconstrained` outstanding count, records exactly one flip, and wakes workers only for non-empty
work. An empty flip is still a generation transition and is recorded exactly once.

Workers poll the active prioritized lane before its normal lane, then use P3.1's unchanged local
refill, preferred-worker attribution, stealing, queued-to-executing ownership transition, callback,
and completion accounting. The producer and sealed sides are never polled.

## Lazy and eager behavior

Lazy callers submit the decrement packet to the prioritized producer side. The packet is invisible
during the pause and becomes executable only after resume and publication. Eager/lazy-off callers
submit the same payload and callback through P3.1's `Submit` to `STWRCDecsAndSweep`, so execution is
inside the pause; the corresponding deferred flip is empty.

Packets continue to carry `uintptr_t` references. P3.3 does not encode RC counts and therefore does
not hardcode a width incompatible with P2.1's selectable 2/4/8-bit metadata or the default
`MAX_REF_COUNT == 3`.

## Reset, shutdown, and failure recovery

`ResetCompletedCycle` requires publication and a drained active side. It returns `Busy` while any
published packet is global, locally cached, or executing. Early, stale, wrong-generation, and
duplicate publications fail without changing indices or ownership.

Shutdown drains producer, sealed, active-global, bucket-global, and worker-local queues. Every
queued packet returns to caller ownership exactly once with generation and preferred-worker state
cleared. Already executing callbacks finish under the existing P3.1 shutdown/join protocol.

## Integration boundary

Product sources are compiled for P3.3 only with
`CLR_CMAKE_ENABLE_LXR_DEFERRED_BUCKET_TEST=1`. The option defaults off. It exposes only
`GC_LxrDeferredBucketTest_Run` for linked and standalone validation. The shared packet and scheduler
translation units are included once if either the P3.1 or P3.3 validation option is selected.
Normal Workstation/Server builds contain no P3.3 smoke export or call site.

P3.3 does not import P3.2 pause-kind policy and does not implement P3.4 completion counters,
next-GC decisions, finalizable promotion, or block release.

## Validation and benchmark boundary

The direct validators cover disabled mode, both queue directions, visibility before and immediately
after publication, priority, exactly-once and empty flips, generation binding, producer/seal races,
lazy-off placement, repeated cycles, stealing, busy recovery, and shutdown ownership. Linked and
standalone smoke runs Workstation and Server modes.

The benchmark uses persistent workers, preallocated 1024-reference packets, identical payloads and
callbacks, interleaved eager/lazy arms, and matched callback/checksum totals. The measured synthetic
pause is `BeginStwCycle` through STW completion. Lazy concurrent completion is deliberately outside
that interval but inside total-cycle accounting. Same-code A/A, zero-pass/null, and 64-extra-pass
injected controls establish sensitivity.

These are scheduler/substrate measurements. No complete LXR collector exists, so the results are
not LXR-versus-Workstation/Server measurements and are not compared with paper pause tables as
parity evidence.

## Candidate validation and measurement

The pre-freeze source passes 357/357 direct checks on Windows x64 and Linux x64. The P3.1 validator
continues to pass 642/642 checks. The option-enabled Windows x64 Debug product builds, and linked
plus standalone smoke passes in Workstation and Server modes, four rows total. A separate default-off
Windows x64 Debug build contains the validation export in neither `coreclr.dll` nor `clrgc.dll`.
The P2.2, P3.1, and P3.3 validation options also build together. The packet and scheduler translation
units compile with the Windows x86 and ARM64 target toolchains; this is compile evidence, not
execution evidence.

The candidate benchmark uses three launches, four warmup cycles, 64 measured cycles, 32 preallocated
packets per cycle, and 1024 references per packet. All 105 rows and five per-worker controls pass.
Mean synthetic pause duration is shown in nanoseconds:

| workers | lazy | eager | eager/lazy | A/A | injected separation |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 15,366 | 71,494 | 4.65 | 0.861 | 25.65 |
| 2 | 16,913 | 50,844 | 3.01 | 1.064 | 45.46 |
| 4 | 34,519 | 56,202 | 1.63 | 1.002 | 24.86 |
| 8 | 68,433 | 158,531 | 2.32 | 1.046 | 8.50 |
| 16 | 131,114 | 333,781 | 2.55 | 0.823 | 2.94 |

“Injected separation” is the eager injected/base ratio divided by the lazy injected/base ratio. It
demonstrates that the fixed extra callback work enters the eager pause but remains outside the lazy
pause. The zero-pass rows characterize the timer/scheduler floor; they are not subtracted to create
a manufactured collector number. At 16 workers the eager base mean is slightly below its eager-null
mean, an explicit reminder that these small absolute intervals are noisy even though the injected
control is large. The result is evidence that deferral moves matched callback work out of this
synthetic pause boundary, not a claim about application-observed latency.

Two pilot policies are retained as superseded attempts. Eight measured cycles were too short:
one-worker A/A was 0.187 and the lazy injected/base ratio was 14.8. A 64-cycle pilot stabilized A/A,
but its initial per-arm lazy injected upper bound still failed at one worker (1.85) even while eager
injected/base was 61.25. The final control therefore tests the mechanism directly: eager injected
cost must be at least 1.25x and at least twice the corresponding lazy ratio. It does not require a
noisy, near-zero lazy pause ratio to equal one.
